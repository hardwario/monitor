The fifth release candidate of **HARDWARIO Monitor**, the console, log and flashing tool for HARDWARIO CHESTER, as a desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux and as the command-line tool `hwio`. It builds on 2.0.0-rc.4: guides for setup, connecting, troubleshooting and flashing, firmware for CHESTER's LoRaWAN module and for STM32L0 chips, AI assistants in remote support, and fixes for what was reported while testing rc.4.

This is a pre-release for testing. Please report problems in [Issues](https://github.com/hardwario/monitor/issues); `hwio diag report` (or **Help › Create diagnostic report…** in the app) creates a report you can attach. HARDWARIO Monitor version 1 stays available and can be installed side by side until version 2 replaces it.

## What is new since 2.0.0-rc.4

**Guides (wizards)**
- New: the **setup wizard** (J-Link Software, USB access on Linux, nRF Util, a test of the probes), the **Connect** wizard (probe, cable, power, a live check, connect), **Troubleshooting** (choose the problem; checks and steps) and the **guided flash** of application firmware. They are in **Help**, behind **Help me fix this** of an error, and on the Choose device and Flash pages (**Guide me**).
- They open in the page area of the tab, like the LTE update guide: the tabs, the device bar and the rail stay; **Wizard** in the rail brings a running guide back. Every device tab can run its own guide.
- The setup wizard opens at the first start. Its first step now asks **How do you want to use HARDWARIO Monitor?** (Basic, Advanced, Expert); there is no separate mode question any more. Change the mode later in **Settings › General › Mode**.
- **Every 2.0.0-rc.4 user sees the setup wizard once** after updating (rc.4 did not record it); the mode you chose in rc.4 stays and is not asked again.
- On a support session tab the Connect, Troubleshooting and guided flash wizards do not open: they work with a device connected to your own computer.
- The setup wizard's first step also chooses the **Language**, and its probe step offers **Update J-Link firmware …** for a probe with outdated firmware.
- The diagnostic report, the keyboard shortcuts, the J-Link firmware update, **Join support session…** and **Share with support…** open in the page area too, instead of dialogs.

**LoRaWAN module of CHESTER**
- The Flash page has a third card, **LoRaWAN module** (Murata Type ABZ): it flashes **lora-modem for CHESTER 1.4.1 (recommended)**, the open LoRaWAN modem firmware by HARDWARIO, or a `.hex` / `.bin` file of your own. The recommended firmware is downloaded from GitHub on first use, checked by its SHA-256 checksum and kept in the cache.
- **Flash** opens a guide like the LTE update: J-Link on the APP connector, the application is stopped, move the cable to the LoRaWAN connector, flash, move it back. The console of the tab is paused meanwhile (**Paused for the LoRaWAN module**).
- A factory module has Murata's original firmware with read-out protection: the guide asks **Unlock the LoRaWAN module?** first. Unlocking erases the module, and the original Murata firmware **cannot be backed up or written back**.
- In the Expert mode, **Maintenance** also lists **LoRaWAN module** with **Erase chip…** and **Unlock (erase)…**. See [LoRaWAN module](https://github.com/hardwario/monitor/blob/main/docs/en/flashing.md#lorawan-module).

**STM32 chips**
- New device **STM32 chip**: HARDWARIO Monitor flashes STM32L0 chips over the J-Link's SWD pins, for example the controller of a CHESTER extension board. Choose the part under **Chip** (for example **STM32L0 cat. 3, 64 KiB**).
- The Flash page takes a `.hex` or `.bin` file (a `.bin` needs its **Start address**), with **Erase the whole chip first**, **Keep data EEPROM** (on by default) and **Halt the CPU after flashing**. The chip is identified before writing; another chip than the chosen one stops the flash and offers **Use <found chip>**.
- A chip with read-out protection level 1 offers **Unlock…**, which erases it completely, including the data EEPROM. **Maintenance** has **Reset**, **Reset and halt**, **Erase chip…** and **Unlock (erase)…**. An STM32 chip tab has no console.
- **CHESTER-S1 (Clime IAQ):** HARDWARIO has no prepared firmware for its controller yet; back it up before you flash or erase it. See [Flashing STM32 chips](https://github.com/hardwario/monitor/blob/main/docs/en/flashing.md#stm32-chips).

**Console**
- The command input and the log's search bar have the same height and line up side by side (#5).
- The log's search bar stays one row: an active level or text filter is a small **✕** right of the level buttons, and "Showing X of Y lines" is shown inside the search field (#6).
- **More › Save device information…** saves what the device reports with `info show` and `config show` into one text file named by its serial number, for example `2159018267_2026-11-25_141502.txt` (J-Link and Bluetooth tabs, not remote support tabs). The commands do not appear in the console; secrets are written as `<redacted>` unless you tick **Include secrets**, and on Linux and macOS only your user can read the file. See [Console](https://github.com/hardwario/monitor/blob/main/docs/en/console.md#save-device-information).

**LTE firmware update**
- The steps with the J-Link cable show a drawing of the CHESTER with the APP or LTE connector highlighted (#7).
- **Cancel** after the application was erased asks **Write the application back first?**: **Write back** puts the saved copy back (connect the J-Link to APP when asked), **Leave it erased** ends as before (#8).
- The application firmware card (Flash page and the guide) offers the saved copies of the device (**Application copy from …**) and **Open backups folder** (#8).
- An error inside the guide no longer shows buttons that do nothing; it links to **Troubleshooting** for that error, and **Try again** at the bottom goes on (#9).
- A failure in the guided update keeps its real cause: too low supply voltage reported by nRF Util is now *The LTE MCU reports too low supply voltage.* (`target.low_voltage`), and the advice is to check the power supply and the cable, not to use the guided update (#9).
- When the guide's content is longer than the window, the scroll bar stays visible and a fade shows that more is below; choosing **Firmware from the catalog or a file** scrolls the picker into view (#10).

**Flash page**
- Recent catalog firmware shows its name and version (for example *hio-chester-meteo v4.0.1*) instead of the 32-digit ID; older entries get their names when the list is shown (#11).

**Privacy**
- **Save view…** writes the claim token, the BLE passkey and commands with secret arguments as `<redacted>`, as the console files do, and on Linux and macOS only your user can read the saved file. The screen and **Copy** still show the values.
- The diagnostic report replaces the address of your computer's Bluetooth adapter with `<adapter address>`; the app and `hwio doctor` still show it.

**App**
- **The app always starts with one New device tab.** The tabs of the last run are no longer reopened, and **Settings › General › Reopen tabs at start** is gone.
- `hwio-monitor --version` prints the version and `hwio-monitor --help` the options, without opening the window.

**AI assistants (MCP)**
- The question an AI client's flash request asks shows the firmware by name and version (the catalog ID small below it), the device, the MCU and the client; the button that remembers the answer is **Always allow flashing for <client>**.
- **Settings › AI assistants (MCP) › Permissions** has a row **All** that sets every permission at once; **Always allow** there asks **Always allow everything?** first.
- Tool results are in English whatever language the app uses. `list_devices` lists only Bluetooth devices with a known profile (`all_ble: true` lists all, `ble_hidden` counts the rest). Flash results say whether anything was written (`unchanged`, `affected_bytes`) and how consent was given (`consent`). `read_registers` takes `halt: true`.
- New tools for STM32 chips: `stm32_info`, `stm32_flash`, `stm32_erase`, `stm32_unlock`.

**Remote support**
- In a support session tab, the device bar's button is now **Leave session**: it leaves the session and keeps the tab with its console, so you can still save it. Afterwards the button is **Join again** (the customer is asked to allow you again). While an operation you asked for runs, the button waits. The banner's action that closes the tab is now called **Close tab**.
- The customer sees when support leaves: after **Leave session**, closing the support tab or quitting, the customer's console shows *— Remote: Support left the session. —*, the banner no longer says that support can send commands, and commands are not accepted until the customer allows support again. An operation already running finishes. Both sides need 2.0.0-rc.5 or newer; with an older Monitor, the web Terminal or the mobile app nothing changes.
- A session shared from the **HARDWARIO mobile app** shows the whole answer of a command in the history and live, instead of blank lines or only the prompt; a line cut between two Bluetooth messages is shown whole, and the prompt is not shown as a line. The tab is titled *Session NNN NNN — web Terminal or mobile app*, and when the customer stops sharing in the app, the session ends in HARDWARIO Monitor too.
- Secrets a customer's web Terminal or mobile app sends unhidden (claim token, BLE passkey, LoRaWAN keys) are hidden before they reach support's console, a saved view and `hwio remote join`. A customer's HARDWARIO Monitor now also hides a key in the form `config show` prints it (`lrw config appkey <redacted>`), as the console file does.
- The application log has counts and lengths of the console lines a session forwards and receives (never their text), to find where a line lost its text.

**AI assistants (MCP) in remote support**
- Support can let an AI assistant work with a customer's device in a joined support session: turn on **Settings › AI assistants (MCP) › AI clients may use remote support sessions** (off by default). The AI client sees the tab as `remote:<n>`, never with the session code. Its first use in a session asks you (**Allow for this session** or **Deny**; never remembered), and the tab's banner shows **AI client active: …** with **Stop AI**.
- The AI client reads the console with secret values and the session code hidden (`<redacted>`, `<session>`), sends commands (one at a time, at most 30 a minute; a command that sets a key is refused), and asks for a catalog flash, the LTE update or a reset. Each operation asks you every time and the customer confirms it as your request; on the Flash page it shows *AI: …* with **Stop waiting**, which ends the AI call while the operation finishes on the customer's side. You answer the LTE update's questions, never the AI client.
- Nothing changes for the customer: the AI client's commands and requests appear as support's. The customer's console text reaches the AI client's provider only when support turns this on and allows it; see [MCP server](https://github.com/hardwario/monitor/blob/main/docs/en/mcp.md#remote-support-sessions) and Privacy. New MCP error ids `remote.command_not_delivered`, `remote.command_refused` and `remote.waiting_for_customer`.

## Command line and scripts

- New `hwio chester lrw info|flash|erase|unlock|reset` for the LoRaWAN module. `flash` without a file flashes lora-modem for CHESTER 1.4.1 and guides you through the cable moves; `--direct` skips the guide when the J-Link is already on the LoRaWAN connector and the application MCU is halted. `erase` and `unlock` need `--yes` without a terminal.
- New `hwio stm32 chips|info|flash|erase|unlock|reset --chip ID` for STM32L0 chips (`flash`: `--address`, `--erase-all`, `--allow-eeprom`, `--halt`). `erase`, `unlock` and `flash --erase-all` need `--yes` without a terminal.
- New `hwio chester app regs --halt` and `hwio chester lte regs --halt`: the CPU is halted for the read and runs again afterwards. `regs` also returned wrong values for some registers; they are correct now.
- Flash commands say when nothing had to be written (*Flash contents already matched; nothing was programmed.*); `--json` adds `unchanged` and `affected_bytes`.
- New `hwio chester app info save [PATH]` and `hwio ble info save [PATH]` write the same device information file (`--with-secrets` writes the secrets; `--json` gives the path, the serial number and which commands answered). An existing file is never replaced (`io.file_exists`, exit code 3).
- New `hwio chester app backups [--serial N]` lists the application copies saved by the guided LTE update; `hwio chester app flash --backup latest|PATH` flashes one (a copy of another device needs `--force`).
- `hwio chester lte flash --guided`: Ctrl+C after the erase asks `Write the application back first? [Y/n]`.
- New error codes `target.low_voltage`, `target.wrong_chip`, `target.permanently_protected`, `target.unlock_failed` (exit code 6), `firmware.bin_address_required` (exit code 7) and `remote.target_unsupported`; see Troubleshooting.
- **Changed exit codes:** a firmware file that does not exist (`hwio chester app flash FILE`, `hwio chester lte flash FILE`, `hwio chester app flash --backup PATH`) is now `io.file_not_found` with exit code 3 (was `usage.invalid_argument`, 2), as the documented exit codes say. An unknown command with `--help` (for example `hwio rtt --help`) is now a usage error with exit code 2 instead of printing the main help with 0.

## Known limitations of this release candidate

- Remote support: a web Terminal customer offers support the console only (no flash, erase, recover or reset).
- Remote support with several participants: when one of them leaves, the customer's Monitor cannot tell who it was, so the others need the customer's permission again too (they get it by joining again).
- An AI client in a support session cannot cancel an operation on the customer's device (it can only stop waiting), cannot erase or recover it, and `hwio mcp` cannot join a session. The session code is guessable, which is why the console output is visible to anyone with the code but commands always need your approval.
- Remote support does not flash, erase or unlock the LoRaWAN module, and an STM32 chip tab cannot be shared (it has no console).
- LoRaWAN module: lora-modem for CHESTER 1.4.1 is the only prepared firmware. When the guide is cancelled after it stopped the application, the application stays stopped until CHESTER is reset or powered off and on.
- STM32 chips: STM32L0 only; the STM32L010 value line is not covered.
- **Help me fix this** of the error *The MCU is read-back protected.* (`target.protected`) on an STM32 chip or the LoRaWAN module opens a Troubleshooting topic that describes **Recover** of the nRF chips; use **Unlock…** (or `hwio stm32 unlock`, `hwio chester lrw unlock`) instead.
- A plain `hwio chester lte flash` (without `--guided`) can fail while the application firmware is running, because it may reset the LTE MCU during the update; `hwio` warns about this. Use `hwio chester lte flash --guided` or **Flash › Update LTE firmware…**, which stop the application first.
- The pictures of the APP and LTE connectors in the LTE update guide are schematic drawings, and the pictures for the LoRaWAN connector and the STM32 wiring are placeholders; photos come later.
- While the setup wizard is open it covers every tab; a guide running in a tab goes on underneath and is shown again when the setup wizard closes.
- nRF Util is not installed by the app yet; follow the steps behind **How to install ↗**.
- `hwio chester app halt` halts the CPU only while the command runs; it is running again after the command exits. To read the registers use `regs --halt`.
- The app does not update itself; install a newer version over the old one, settings are kept.
